# Lecture 1: Membrane structure and transport

A phospholipid has a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails. In water, phospholipids form a bilayer with the heads facing aqueous environments and the tails facing inward.

Small non-polar molecules can cross the bilayer by simple diffusion. Ions and many polar molecules require membrane proteins. Facilitated diffusion moves a substance down its concentration or electrochemical gradient and does not directly consume ATP. Active transport moves a substance against a gradient and requires an energy source.

Cholesterol buffers membrane fluidity: it restrains phospholipid movement at higher temperatures and prevents tight packing at lower temperatures.
